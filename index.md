# Reza

I'm a researcher working at the intersection of machine learning, security, and the physical world. This site is a quick tour of where I've been and what I'm working on now.

---

## Now — PhD in automotive cybersecurity

I'm a PhD researcher on the **EPSRC CDT** programme, focused on the **privacy and security of automotive Telematics Control Units (TCUs)** and the communication channels around them. A TCU is the gateway between a vehicle and the outside world, which makes it both a high-value attack surface and a privacy minefield.

My approach combines:

- **Privacy-preserving techniques** so sensitive vehicle and driver data never has to leave the car in raw form
- **Federated learning**, so detection models can be trained across a fleet without centralising the data
- **Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)** trained on in-vehicle network traffic to flag anomalies in real time

The project is funded by **EPSRC** and **BEAM Connectivity Ltd**.

---

## Previously — ML for light microscopy (Swansea)

Before the CDT, I spent a year on a PhD at Swansea University's Chemistry department, asking a deliberately cheeky question: can machine learning substitute for expensive optics? In other words — can you reach the image quality of tens-of-thousands-of-pounds lenses with cheaper hardware and smarter post-processing?

The rig used **Hamamatsu** cameras, **interferometer-based per-pixel colour detection**, and **light processors from Texas Instruments** driven by voltage signalling, to resolve colour at fine intensity levels.

## MSc — Human Big Data & Artificial Intelligence

My master's project was on **semantic segmentation of pancreatic tissue in abdominal CT scans**, aimed at supporting earlier detection of pancreatic cancer — one of the very few cancers whose survival rate has barely shifted in over forty years. Anything that nudges that number deserves attention.

---

## BSc — Medical Genetics

My undergraduate degree was in medical genetics: biology's version of coding, where the "language" is proteins and the "compiler" is the cell. My final-year project **automated the micronucleus assay**, the gold-standard test in genotoxicology, removing much of the manual scoring bottleneck that slows the technique down.

---

## The through-line

Different domains, same instinct: take a slow, expensive, expert-bound process and see how far automation, signal processing, and machine learning can push it — without losing the rigour the original experts brought to it.
